The Slow Harvest Chronicle

Wild lotus ponds of North Bihar, hand-harvested at dawn.

Makhana (Euryale ferox) is harvested from pristine, still wetland pools in the historical Mithila belt. Harvesters dive into fresh sediment to hand-collect black thorny seeds, which are spread along sunlit riverbanks for seven days of gentle natural curing.

Unlike industrial flash-fried snack alternatives, our seeds are roasted over slow mango-wood fires in hemispherical clay pans, then popped individually by hand with wooden mallets. The result is pure, featherlight expansion with zero oil residue and an intoxicating natural nuttiness.

Preserving Crispness & Vitality

Guidelines from our packaging artisans

Airtight Jar Transfer: While our ziplock pouch provides barrier protection against ambient humidity, for optimal crunch beyond 30 days, decant into a dry ceramic or glass pantry jar.

Cool, Dry Sanctuary: Keep away from steamy stoves and direct solar exposure. Ideal storage temperature is below 24°C in a low-moisture cupboard.

Reviving Ambient Humidity: If exposed to rainy monsoon air, simply warm gently in a dry cast iron pan for 90 seconds without oil to restore pristine, bone-dry crunch.

calendar_today Shelf Life: 6 months from packaging date printed on the pouch seam.
